A Supreme Leader Killed, a Son Installed,
and No End in Sight
When US-Israeli strikes killed Ayatollah Ali Khamenei on February 28, Washington expected a regime without a head to collapse. Instead, the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps drove a succession in nine days — installing the slain leader's son — and Iran shut the Strait of Hormuz to the world. Day 15, no ceasefire.
2026 Iran War — Operation Epic Fury
US-Israel strikes kill Supreme Leader · Strait of Hormuz closed · Succession crisis
On February 28, 2026, the United States and Israel launched coordinated airstrikes on Iran. Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei, dozens of senior officials, and his family were killed. Iran responded by closing the Strait of Hormuz — through which 20% of world oil flows — striking 9 countries, and selecting Mojtaba Khamenei, son of the slain leader, as the third Supreme Leader on March 9. No ceasefire as of March 15.

Constitutional architecture · Velayat-e Faqih
The Circular Lock: How Every Path to Power
Leads Back to the Supreme Leader
Iran's government combines a modern republic — elected president, parliament, courts — with a theocratic superstructure rooted in Velayat-e Faqih. The Supreme Leader sits above all three branches. The Guardian Council vets all candidates. The Assembly of Experts selects the Supreme Leader. The Supreme Leader appoints the Guardian Council. The circle is closed.

State doctrine · 1979–present
What the Islamic Republic Calls Itself —
and What Others Call It
Every state has a governing philosophy — a doctrine that justifies its authority. Iran calls its system Velayat-e Faqih, the guardianship of the Islamic jurist. Contested theologically within Islam, declared illegitimate by Iranian opposition, framed as imperialism by Arab neighbors. FORA records all names.
Endonym · What they call themselves
Velayat-e Faqih — Guardianship of the Islamic Jurist
Developed by Khomeini before 1979: in the absence of the Twelfth Imam, legitimate political authority belongs to a qualified Islamic jurist. Constitutionally entrenched — not a policy preference but the legal foundation of the Republic. The Supreme Leader's authority is simultaneously political and religious.

Non-governmental power layer
The Axis of Resistance, Degraded but Not Dismantled
Iran's regional strategy has relied for four decades on proxy militias — organizations it funds, arms, and commands, giving it influence across Lebanon, Gaza, Yemen, and Iraq without direct Iranian troops on foreign soil. The 2024–2026 period has severely degraded this network. Whether it can regenerate is one of the central open questions of the war.
