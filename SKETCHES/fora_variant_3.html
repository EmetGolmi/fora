Variant Set III — The Civic Record

Charter Mark · Terracotta Tile · Hearth Card
Meeting Notice · Welcome Sign · Color Handout

The third register: civic components styled as permanent public record. Not the warm commons (Set I) nor the gathered institution (Set II) — but the published document that will outlast all of us. Libre Baskerville carries the weight of the broadsheet. Newsprint ground. Double-rule borders. The vote count belongs in the archive.

Register
Editorial broadsheet — civic record as document of standing
Ground
Newsprint #f5f2eb — warm, archival, not clinical white
Type voice
Baskerville-dominant — kickers, labels, and score numerals all in serif
What it says
This is the record. Not a notification. Not a feed. The permanent civic account.
Component 01

Status Pills & Tags — Charter Mark

Variant III

Libre Baskerville serif with a 4px rounded corner — the hand-stamped mark of a local charter or a community land trust document. These look like they belong on a printed civic docket, not a SaaS dashboard.

Status tags · Charter Mark — Baskerville, 4px radius, 2pt border
H.R. 4471 — Infrastructure & Rural Broadband Act of 2025 Follow
In Committee Enacted Floor Scheduled Vetoed
The 4px corner radius is a deliberate half-measure — rounded enough to feel designed, square enough to feel official. Baskerville gives status labels documentary gravity. "In Committee" in this face reads like a clerk's stamp on a filed brief. "Enacted" reads like a headline in the Congressional Record.

Component 03

Legitimacy Score — Hearth Card

Variant III · Gilt cream tint

Gilt cream tint — the warmest legitimacy treatment, but here it reads as archival amber rather than domestic warmth. The score and bar both use Federal Gilt, tying the legitimacy signal to the platform's civic authority colour.

Legitimacy score · Hearth Card — gilt cream, gilt score
Legitimacy Citations scored · not claims
78  / 100
Federal Gilt `#b8860b` for both bar fill and score numeral — the legitimacy signal uses the same colour as the Capitol dome. The cream background is archival amber on the newsprint page ground, not a pastel. The italic Baskerville note "Citations scored · not claims" is the civic education embedded in the component — it belongs in this serif register.
Component 04

Steel-Man Banner — Neighbor's Note

Variant I · Royal Phoenician Purple — permanent across all sets

Unchanged across all three sets. Purple is the earned mark of civic valour on this platform — the one colour reserved for the act of presenting the strongest opposing case. It appears here and nowhere else.

Steel-man challenge · Neighbor's Note — Phoenician purple, permanent across all sets
⚖️
Steel-man challenge
Before posting, articulate the strongest case for the view you oppose. Not a weakened version — the best version. The one a thoughtful advocate would make.
Royal Phoenician — reserved for earned civic valour across the platform
In the editorial register, the steel-man is an *editor's challenge to the writer*: you have not yet earned the right to dismiss the opposing argument. Present it first at full strength. The purple left-rule is the only asymmetric border element in this otherwise column-structured set, which gives it additional visual weight.
Component 05

Tone Intercept — Meeting Notice

Variant III · White · Top border · Editorial restraint

The most restrained of the three tone treatments. White card, gilt top border — an editor's note pinned to the copy before it goes to print. No fill, no alarm, no colour field. The civic expectation stated plainly.

Tone intercept · Meeting Notice — white, 3pt gilt top border
🌡
Tone check
Your draft may read as dismissive of diaspora perspectives. The civic record is a place of good-faith exchange — consider revising before posting.
The white field on the newsprint ground creates the strongest contrast in this set — making the Meeting Notice more visible precisely because it uses restraint while everything else uses tint. The gilt header matches the Hearth Card legitimacy treatment — both use gilt as the civic authority signal. "Post anyway" is always accessible: the platform educates, it does not censor.

Component 07

Civic Scaffold Cards — Color Handout

Variant III · Serif kickers

Background tint cards with italic Baskerville kickers — the colour handouts passed around at a public hearing. Each card is a self-contained document. The kicker italic gives it the feel of margin annotations in a published brief.

Civic scaffold · Color Handout — background tint, Baskerville italic kickers
What it does
Shifts housing from state assignment to cooperative ownership for residents with 10+ years of continuous occupancy.
Transfiere la vivienda de asignación estatal a propiedad cooperativa.
Fiscal note
₱840M in title transfer revenue over 5 years. Implementation cost ~₱120M. Net positive by year 3.
₱840M en ingresos · costo ₱120M.
Best case
Builds property-owning citizenry. Incentivizes maintenance. State housing burden −30% within a decade.
Construye ciudadanía propietaria. Carga estatal −30%.
Worst case
Wealthier residents consolidate multiple units. Long-term low-income tenants priced out at cooperative pricing thresholds.
Los ricos consolidan. Los inquilinos pobres quedan fuera.
The Baskerville italic kicker followed by a thin underrule creates a typographic hierarchy borrowed from the academic brief: *heading*, then body. The kicker colour and the background tint provide two independent signals of the card's type — satisfying WCAG 1.4.1 at both the text and background level. Best/worst use full tint; informational cards use white to reduce visual weight.
