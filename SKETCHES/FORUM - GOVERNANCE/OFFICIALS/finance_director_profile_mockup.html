Philadelphia's chief financial officer — appointed, not elected. Dubow doesn't decide how money is spent — that's the Mayor and Council. He makes sure there's money to spend. He manages the city's $6.84B budget, keeps the books, issues bonds, manages the pension fund, and tells the Mayor when the numbers don't work. When something gets cut, he's usually the one who identified the problem first.

What this office does
Proposes and manages the annual budget
Works with every city department to build the Mayor's budget proposal. Monitors spending throughout the year and flags overruns to the Mayor and Council.
Borrows money on behalf of the city
Issues municipal bonds to fund capital projects — new buildings, infrastructure, housing. The city's credit rating determines the interest rate. Higher rating = lower borrowing cost = more money for services.
Manages the pension fund
Oversees $7B+ in pension obligations for city workers. Currently at ~68% funded — up from 44.8% in 2016. When fully funded, the city saves $430M/year in annual contributions.
Keeps the books for the entire city
All city financial activity — every contract, payment, and grant — flows through his office. Publishes the Comprehensive Annual Financial Report (CAFR).
Monitors federal funding risk
In 2024, Philly received $2.8B in federal grants. Any reduction — Medicaid, housing, education — hits the city budget immediately. Dubow tracks this and builds reserves.

Current fiscal risks — as of March 2026
High
Federal funding cuts
$2.8B in federal grants at risk from Washington policy changes. A $95M reserve has been set aside. Education, medical institutions, and federal jobs generate $1B+ in wage tax revenue alone.
High
ARPA funds exhausted
$1.4B in pandemic federal relief is gone as of end of 2024. Programs funded by ARPA must now come from city tax revenue or be cut.
Med
Pension fund not yet fully funded
At ~68% funded. Annual contributions are a fixed cost. Full funding projected within the decade — at which point $430M/year is freed up.
Med
Commercial property tax base erosion
Remote work has reduced commercial real estate values. Lower assessments = lower property tax revenue from office buildings.
