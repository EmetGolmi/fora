Iran War · Day 18 · March 17 2026
US–Israel strikes on Iran — the most significant Middle East war since 2003

On February 28 2026, the United States and Israel launched coordinated strikes on Iran, targeting its nuclear infrastructure, ballistic missile program, and military command-and-control. The operation — called "Operation Epic Fury" by US CENTCOM — began hours after Oman's Foreign Minister announced a diplomatic "breakthrough" was imminent, with Iran having agreed to nuclear concessions. Trump said he was "not thrilled" with the negotiations and chose force instead.

Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei was killed in the opening strikes. His son Mojtaba Khamenei was elected as his successor on March 8 by the Assembly of Experts — the same body that was bombed by Israel while in session on March 3. Trump called the new supreme leader "lightweight" and threatened he would not last long without US approval.

Iran has retaliated with waves of ballistic missiles and drones targeting Israel and US military bases across at least 12 countries in the region. Iran has closed the Strait of Hormuz to "enemies." The deadliest single incident: a US strike on an elementary school in Minab, Iran, killing at least 170 people — mostly schoolgirls. Amnesty International has confirmed the strike and is investigating it as a potential war crime.

The EU has refused to join US naval operations at Hormuz. Germany: "no intention of joining." Europe has "no appetite" to send troops. Separately, the UK has allowed the US to use British bases at Diego Garcia and RAF Fairford for strikes against Iran's missile infrastructure.

Market context · Iran War Effect
The economic shock of an active Middle East war

The US–Israel war on Iran has become the dominant driver of global market behavior since February 28. The Strait of Hormuz closure — through which 20% of global oil and 25% of LNG passes — pushed Brent crude from ~$70/barrel to a peak of $120/barrel last week. It has since settled around $104, but remains well above pre-war levels.

On March 9 (day 9 of the war), markets saw one of their sharpest single-day declines since the 2020 COVID shock: Japan's Nikkei plunged 7% intraday, closing down 5.2%. Germany's DAX fell 2.6%. The UK's FTSE 100 lost 1.9%. Since then, markets have partially recovered as tankers navigated the strait safely and major economies signaled they would release strategic petroleum reserves.

Energy companies are reaching 52-week highs. APA, BP, ConocoPhillips, Devon Energy all up sharply. The XOP energy ETF has reached all-time highs. Conversely, consumer discretionary, manufacturing, and airlines have been under pressure from oil-driven cost inflation — though airline stocks recovered today on strong guidance from Delta and American.

🛢 Brent Crude Price Shock

Brent crude · USD/barrel · Jan 2026 – Mar 17 2026

Japan, South Korea, and other oil-importing nations are releasing strategic reserves. The EU has decided against joining US naval operations. Saudi Arabia, UAE, and Kuwait are absorbing Iranian drone and missile attacks while their own oil exports are affected.

🌡 CO₂ · The Keeling Curve

Atmospheric CO₂ · ppm · Mauna Loa Observatory · 1960–2026

Atmospheric CO₂ as measured at Mauna Loa Observatory is approximately 424 ppm in March 2026. Pre-industrial baseline: 280 ppm. The 350 ppm threshold was crossed in 1988. The 400 ppm threshold was crossed in 2013. The seasonal wobble visible in recent years reflects the annual cycle of Northern Hemisphere plant growth absorbing CO₂ in summer and releasing it in winter — the planet breathing.

The 1.5°C Paris Agreement target is now considered essentially locked in by most climate scientists. The Iran war creates an ironic climate dynamic: higher oil prices suppress demand and may accelerate the energy transition — but strategic petroleum reserve releases and energy security anxiety may delay renewables investment in several economies.
