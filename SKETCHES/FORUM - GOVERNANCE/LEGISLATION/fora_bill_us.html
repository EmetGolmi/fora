What it does
Allocates $42 billion over seven years to expand broadband infrastructure in rural and underserved communities. Requires ISPs receiving federal funds to offer a $30/month affordable tier. Establishes a new Rural Connectivity Office within the FCC and mandates 25 Mbps minimum download speeds for all funded projects.
Who it affects
Approximately 21 million Americans in rural areas who currently lack access to broadband. Affects ISPs who bid for federal contracts and the 14 states with less than 50% broadband coverage.
Fiscal note
$42B authorized over FY2026–2032. CBO scores net cost at $38.4B after projected tax revenue from new economic activity. Funded through discretionary appropriations.
Best case
Closes the digital divide for 21 million Americans. Enables telehealth, remote work, and digital education in rural communities. Estimated $64B in long-term economic returns per CBO supplemental analysis.
Worst case
ISPs capture contracts without meaningful rural deployment. Price floor requirements go unenforced. Infrastructure built but unaffordable. Prior rural broadband programs (e.g. FCC E-Rate, 2010 USDA grants) saw similar deployment gaps.
Historical precedent
The Rural Electrification Act of 1936 brought electricity to 90% of American farms within 20 years using a similar federal loan/grant structure. By contrast, the 2010 National Broadband Plan fell short of its targets, largely due to weak deployment mandates. This bill's mandatory service tiers and FCC oversight represent a structural improvement — though enforcement authority remains contested.
