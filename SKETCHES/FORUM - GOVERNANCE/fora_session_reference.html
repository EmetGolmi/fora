Wireframe Roadmap · 01

Screen Roadmap

7 screens total. 1 done. The remaining 6 are sequenced by dependency — each screen unlocks decisions needed for the next.

✓ Completed
Bill Page — Split Layout
The design anchor for the product. Establishes the split-column pattern, bilingual system, civic scaffold, discourse feed, tone intercept, and source quality system. All future screens inherit from this.
File
fora_bill_page_editorial.html
Key decisions locked
Split C layout · EN/ES bilingual display · Scaffold expanded by default · Tone intercept placement
→ Next up
Official Profile
The verified official page. Mirror of the Bill Page in structure — left side is the civic record (biography, voting history, term info, committee memberships), right side is the discourse + Q&A feed. Needs the official badge system and the "Post as Official" CTA for verified account holders.
🏛
Civic Resume integration: How does the official's BAML-parsed CivicProfile surface on this page? Full CV tab or inline summary?
✅
Verified badge treatment: How prominent is the official verification? Twitter-style checkmark, or a full "Official Account" banner?
📊
Voting record display: Does the voting history use a timeline, a filtered table, or a chart? How does it handle abstentions and absences?
💬
Q&A vs. discourse: Officials get a different right-column than citizens — Q&A where constituents ask questions and officials respond. How does this differ from standard discourse?
Later
Home Feed
The personalized civic wall. Populated after address lookup → government stack → follow prompt. Cards surface bills, official activity, sentiment shifts, and upcoming elections from the user's full government stack.
🃏
Card taxonomy: How many card types are there? Bill card, official action card, election card, sentiment alert card — do they all look different or share a unified shell?
📍
Jurisdiction filter: Users follow officials across many levels (federal → local). Does the feed have a jurisdiction rail or is everything blended with tags?
🆕
First-visit empty state: The address lookup → stack → follow flow determines whether the feed is populated. How do we handle the zero-state gracefully?
Later
Government Stack
The address lookup result — the full stack from President to school board. This is the entry moment for every new user and arguably the most important first impression in the product. Designed in v2 entry wireframe but needs editorial direction applied.
🗺
Map integration: Does the stack reveal include a live district map or is it purely a list?
📋
Stack depth: The 22-layer LA stack vs. 8-layer NYC stack — how does the UI handle wildly different jurisdiction depths without breaking layout?
Later
Compose + Tone Intercept Flow
The full contribute flow — from blank compose to posted contribution. Includes steel-man prompt modal, source attachment, semi-anonymous identity selection, and the tone intercept at various trigger thresholds. The most interaction-dense wireframe.
🎭
Identity picker UX: "Teacher · 30s · Havana" — how does a user set and change this? Onboarding flow, settings, or inline picker in compose?
🔗
Source attachment: When a user clicks "Add a source" — is it a URL paste, a search, or a FORA-internal source selector?
⏱
Forced pause mechanic: The design doc specifies a forced pause with an opposing article before posting certain content. How does this render — modal, inline expansion, or timed delay?
Later
Civic Resume
The user's civic identity page — occupation, expertise areas, civic participation history. Parsed by BAML from uploaded credentials into a CivicProfile. Shown semi-anonymously in discourse, fully on this page for the account holder.
🪪
BAML parse UI: How does the credential upload and parsing flow surface to the user? Progress state, parsed output review, edit mode?
👁
Public vs. private view: What does another user see when they click your identity in a contribution vs. what you see on your own profile?
Later
Constituency Dashboard (Official Only)
Private view for verified officials. Aggregated constituent sentiment by bill, issue, and demographic cohort. The Twitter/X intelligence layer — officials see their pulse without individual user data. This is the "other customer" of FORA.
📊
Sentiment aggregation granularity: Do officials see sentiment broken down by district, by demographic, by issue area — or all three at once?
🔒
Privacy architecture: How does the UI make clear that individual user identities are never exposed? Trust-building transparency layer needed.
