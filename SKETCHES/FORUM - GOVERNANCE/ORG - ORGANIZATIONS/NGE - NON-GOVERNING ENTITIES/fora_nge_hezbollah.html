حزب الله — Ḥizbu 'llāh
Hezbollah
Also: Hizballah · Hizbullah · Party of God · Islamic Resistance · Al-Muqawamah al-Islamiyyah
A Lebanese Shia Muslim political party and militant organization, founded in 1982 during the Israeli invasion of Lebanon. Backed and funded primarily by Iran through the IRGC Quds Force. Operates as a "state within a state" in Lebanon — running schools, hospitals, social services, and a military force simultaneously. Designated a Foreign Terrorist Organization by the US since 1997 and by 27+ countries. Since the 2024 war with Israel, the organization has lost most of its leadership, approximately 70% of its weapons arsenal, and its longtime secretary-general Hassan Nasrallah. It is currently in a restructuring phase under new secretary-general Naim Qassem.

Plain language
What is Hezbollah?
Hezbollah is a Lebanese organization that is three things simultaneously — and understanding all three is necessary to understand it at all.

1. A political party. Hezbollah has participated in Lebanese government since 1992. As of February 2025, it holds two cabinet posts. Its parliamentary bloc, Loyalty to the Resistance, has held between 12 and 15 seats in Lebanon's parliament. It is a legal political party that negotiates, campaigns, and governs.

2. A social welfare provider. Hezbollah runs hospitals, schools, construction companies, and social services throughout Lebanon — particularly in the Shia-majority south and Beirut's southern suburbs. For hundreds of thousands of Lebanese Shia, Hezbollah is the primary provider of services the state does not deliver. This is the source of its popular legitimacy and the reason it cannot simply be "destroyed" without destroying the communities it serves.

3. A military force. Hezbollah maintains an army — the largest non-state military force in the world outside of active war zones. Before the 2024 war with Israel, it had an estimated 120,000–200,000 rockets and missiles, 40,000–50,000 fighters, and capabilities exceeding those of most national armies in the Middle East. That arsenal was approximately 70% degraded in the 2024 war.

The three roles are inseparable. Iran funds all three. The social services build loyalty. The political arm provides legal legitimacy. The military arm provides leverage. This is by design — and it is why Western countries' attempts to designate only the "military wing" while maintaining dialogue with the "political wing" has been described by Hezbollah itself as a distinction they do not recognize.
Key context
What they call themselves — and what others call them
What they call themselves: حزب الله — Ḥizbu 'llāh — "Party of God." Also: Al-Muqawamah al-Islamiyyah (The Islamic Resistance). They use "resistance" because they frame their existence as defensive — resistance to Israeli occupation of Lebanese territory and to Western imperialism.

What others call them: Hezbollah (English), Hizballah (US government official spelling), Hizbullah (UK), Hezbolá (Spanish). The spelling varies. The entity is the same.

Why this matters: The name you use reflects your frame. Citizens using FORA will encounter both. The platform presents both without preferring either. Understanding that "Hezbollah" and "The Islamic Resistance" refer to the same organization — but that the choice of name reflects the speaker's political position — is civic literacy.

⚠ Restructuring note · February 2026
Wafiq Safa — head of Coordination and Liaison since 1987, considered one of Hezbollah's most influential figures — resigned in February 2026 after disputes with Qassem. His responsibilities were split three ways (security, coordination, political contacts) — a deliberate decentralization to reduce personal vulnerability to Israeli targeting. Two internal factions have emerged: a hardline core calling for escalation, and a pragmatic bloc aligned with Qassem advocating a different approach. Iran is actively managing the restructuring from Tehran.

History
Major Events Timeline
1980s — Founding
1982 Founded
Hezbollah formed — response to Israeli invasion of Lebanon
Israel invades Lebanon to expel the PLO. IRGC dispatches approximately 1,500 Revolutionary Guards to Lebanon's Bekaa Valley. They train and organize Shia militants into what becomes Hezbollah. The founding charter declares Hezbollah's primary goal: "wiping Israel off the map." The organization draws from the theology of Iran's Velayat-e Faqih — the Guardianship of the Islamic Jurist — meaning it answers to Iran's Supreme Leader as a religious obligation.
1983 Attack
US Embassy and Marine barracks bombings — Beirut
April: US Embassy bombed — 63 killed. October: Suicide truck bombs hit US Marine barracks (241 killed) and French paratrooper barracks (58 killed) simultaneously. The largest single-day death toll for the US military since Vietnam. The US and France withdraw from Lebanon. Hezbollah's first major strategic success: demonstrating that suicide bombing could expel superpowers.
299 killed · US/France withdraw from Lebanon
1985 Attack
TWA Flight 847 hijacking
Hezbollah operatives hijack TWA Flight 847. A US Navy diver, Robert Stethem, is murdered and thrown from the plane. 39 American hostages held for 17 days. The hijacking establishes Hezbollah as a global terrorist actor in Western consciousness. Iran's fingerprints are throughout the operation.
1990s — Expansion & Political Entry
1992 Political
Enters Lebanese parliament · Nasrallah becomes Secretary-General
Hezbollah participates in Lebanese parliamentary elections for the first time, winning 8 seats. This begins the "state within a state" strategy — using political legitimacy to protect military operations. Abbas al-Musawi, Nasrallah's predecessor, is assassinated by Israel in February. Nasrallah, 32, takes over and begins a 32-year tenure that transforms the organization.
1994 Attack · Latin America
AMIA bombing — Buenos Aires · 85 killed
Suicide bombing of the AMIA Jewish community center in Buenos Aires — the deadliest terrorist attack in Argentine history and the first major Hezbollah operation outside Lebanon/Middle East. 85 killed, 300+ wounded. Argentine courts eventually indicted Iranian officials and one Hezbollah operative. As of 2026, no one has been convicted. This attack establishes Hezbollah's Latin American network. Iran's fingerprints confirmed by Argentine and US investigations.
85 killed · 300+ wounded · No convictions 32 years later
1997 Designation
US designates Hezbollah a Foreign Terrorist Organization
Clinton administration officially designates Hezbollah under the Antiterrorism and Effective Death Penalty Act of 1996. This makes material support a federal crime. 25 years of US designations will follow — including dozens of individuals, front companies, and financial networks.
2000s — Strategic Victories & Global Spread
2000 Milestone
Israel withdraws from southern Lebanon
After 22 years of occupation, Israel withdraws from southern Lebanon. Hezbollah claims this as a victory — the first time an Arab force had defeated Israel through sustained military resistance. This moment becomes foundational to Hezbollah's legitimacy narrative in Lebanon and across the Arab world. Nasrallah's prestige reaches its peak.
2006 War
34-day war with Israel · Hezbollah claims "divine victory"
Hezbollah kidnaps two Israeli soldiers, triggering a 34-day war. Israel bombs Lebanon extensively but fails to destroy Hezbollah. Despite massive destruction, Nasrallah declares "divine victory" — the fact that Hezbollah survived and fired rockets into northern Israel throughout is sufficient for the narrative. UN Resolution 1701 ends the war but Hezbollah returns to southern Lebanon and rearmed faster than expected. The war establishes Hezbollah's deterrence value against Israel.
~1,200 Lebanese killed · ~165 Israelis killed
2012 Attack · Europe
Burgas bombing — Bulgaria · 5 Israeli tourists killed
Hezbollah bombs a tourist bus at Burgas Airport, killing 5 Israeli tourists and a Bulgarian driver. The bombing triggers the EU to designate Hezbollah's military wing as a terrorist organization — Europe's first significant step, though still only a partial designation. The operation is attributed to Hezbollah's External Security Organization, the same unit responsible for the 1994 AMIA bombing.
5 Israelis + 1 Bulgarian killed
2013–2023 — Syria, Expansion, October 7
2013 Syrian Civil War
Hezbollah deploys fighters to Syria in support of Assad
Nasrallah publicly acknowledges Hezbollah is fighting in Syria alongside Assad. Thousands of fighters deploy over subsequent years, gaining significant combat experience in urban warfare. Hezbollah loses an estimated 1,700 fighters in Syria. The Syria deployment transforms the organization's military doctrine and provides battle-hardened commanders. Israeli analysts describe the Syria experience as giving Hezbollah capabilities "that cannot be bought."
2023 War · Northern Front
Opens northern front after October 7 Hamas attack
The day after Hamas's October 7 attack on Israel, Hezbollah begins firing rockets and drones into northern Israel "in solidarity." This triggers daily cross-border fire that displaces tens of thousands on both sides of the Lebanon-Israel border. Hezbollah escalates throughout 2023–2024, firing an estimated 8,000+ projectiles into northern Israel before the full war begins. Its stated goal: tie down Israeli forces on the northern front while Gaza fights in the south.
2024–2026 — Decimation & Rebuilding
Sep 2024 Assassination
Israel assassinates Nasrallah, Safieddine, and most of Hezbollah's leadership
September 17: Israel detonates pagers used by Hezbollah operatives across Lebanon — thousands wounded, hundreds killed. September 23: Massive Israeli airstrikes across Lebanon. September 27: Nasrallah killed in airstrike on Beirut's southern suburbs after 32 years as leader. October 2024: Hashem Safieddine (presumed successor) also killed. Fuad Shukr, Ibrahim Aqil, Ali Karaki, and most of the first- and second-tier military leadership are eliminated in rapid succession. The connective tissue between Hezbollah and Iran has been severed at the personal level.
Most of Hezbollah's top leadership eliminated · 32-year leadership era ends
Nov 2024 Ceasefire
US-brokered ceasefire · Hezbollah severely degraded
Israel and Hezbollah agree to a ceasefire brokered by the US and France. IDF assessment: ~70% of Hezbollah's weapons arsenal neutralized, ~80% of Radwan Force weapons destroyed, most tunnel network in southern Lebanon destroyed. 90% of Hezbollah infrastructure in south Lebanon dismantled by early 2025. Lebanese Army begins deploying in the south for the first time in decades. Naim Qassem elected Secretary-General on October 29.
Aug 2025 Disarmament
Lebanese government votes to disarm Hezbollah — Homeland Shield Plan
Lebanese government votes to approve Hezbollah disarmament. Lebanese Army presents the Homeland Shield Plan. Hezbollah and Amal ministers walk out in protest. Thousands rally against approval. The plan is based on a US framework. As of March 2026, implementation is slow and contested — the Lebanese Army has entered some former Hezbollah facilities in the south but the organization retains weapons and is actively rebuilding.
Mar 2026 Current
Hezbollah fires missiles at Israel in retaliation for Khamenei killing
March 1, 2026: For the first time since the joint US-Israeli strikes on Iran, Hezbollah launches missiles and drones targeting northern and central Israel in retaliation for the killing of Supreme Leader Khamenei. This breaks the ceasefire. Israeli airstrikes follow on southern Beirut, Bekaa Valley, and Tyre. Lebanese PM Nawaf Salam condemns the attacks as "irresponsible acts outside Lebanese state authority." The March 12 Temple Israel attack in Michigan occurs 11 days after these strikes.

Iran · Islamic Republic
Sponsor · Creator · Command authority
Confirmed
sponsored_by · commands · funds · arms
Iran created Hezbollah in 1982. The relationship is not merely financial — it is theological. Under Velayat-e Faqih, Hezbollah answers to Iran's Supreme Leader as a religious obligation. Iran provides an estimated $700M–$1B annually in pre-2023 funding (significantly reduced after Israeli-US strikes). The IRGC Quds Force is the primary conduit — providing weapons, training, intelligence, and strategic direction. Hezbollah's precision-guided missile program was built with Iranian technical assistance.

The relationship has been complicated by: the death of Soleimani (2020, who was personally managing Hezbollah's regional operations), the death of Khamenei (2026), and Hezbollah's military degradation. Naim Qassem is described as being "backed by Iran" in his consolidation effort — Iran is managing the restructuring from Tehran.

🇻🇪
Venezuela · Maduro regime
Safe haven · Financial hub · Narco nexus
Confirmed (US/Argentine)
operates_within · funds_through · safe_haven
Venezuela under Chavez and then Maduro has been Hezbollah's primary Western Hemisphere base since the early 2000s. The relationship is ideological (shared anti-Americanism), financial (narcotrafficking, gold smuggling, money laundering), and operational (training facilities, passport provision, logistical infrastructure).

Key documented elements: Tareck El Aissami, former Venezuelan VP — indicted by US for narco-terrorism — served as the primary regime-Hezbollah link; Venezuelan passports issued to 10,000+ individuals from Lebanon, Syria, and Iran (2010–2019), many allegedly approved under El Aissami; Hezbollah established a paramilitary training center on Margarita Island (claimed); Iranian gold mining operations in Venezuela's Orinoco Mining Arc provide indirect Hezbollah revenue; Tri-Border Area (Argentina/Brazil/Paraguay) operations use Venezuela as logistics hub.

Current status (2026): The US operation to arrest Maduro in early 2026 has disrupted Hezbollah's Venezuela operations. Approximately 400 Hezbollah commanders reportedly deployed to Venezuela from early 2025 as the Middle East position weakened. The closure of Venezuela as a financial hub is assessed as a significant additional blow to Hezbollah's already-strained finances.

Ally · Coordinated resistance · Axis of Resistance
Confirmed
allied_with · coordinated_with · arms_supplier
Both are members of Iran's "Axis of Resistance." Hezbollah has provided weapons, training, and financing to Hamas. The October 7, 2023 Hamas attack on Israel directly triggered Hezbollah's northern front opening. However, the two organizations have different ideological roots — Hezbollah is Shia, Hamas is Sunni (Muslim Brotherhood tradition) — and their relationship is strategic rather than theological. Hezbollah took significant damage opening a front "in solidarity" with Hamas.

Lebanon · Lebanese state
Host state · Political participant · Contested relationship
Complex · Contested
operates_within · governs_within · contested_by
Hezbollah is simultaneously inside and outside the Lebanese state. It holds cabinet posts. It participates in parliament. It also maintains a military force that the Lebanese state cannot control — which is the central contradiction. PM Nawaf Salam (Feb 2025 government) is the most openly anti-Hezbollah Lebanese PM in decades. He condemned the March 2026 rocket launches as "outside the authority of the Lebanese state." The Homeland Shield Plan (Sep 2025) is the state's attempt to disarm Hezbollah — with very limited progress so far.

Terrorist Designations
27+ countries · varying scope
The distinction between "full organization" and "military wing only" designations is significant. Hezbollah itself says there is no such distinction — the organization is unified. Many European countries maintained the partial distinction for years to preserve diplomatic contacts with the "political wing." The UK and Germany moved to full designation in 2019 and 2020 respectively. The EU still maintains a partial designation (military wing only) as of 2026.

What is confirmed vs. assessed

Some claims — particularly about Venezuela — are confirmed by court documents. Others are assessed by intelligence agencies. FORA labels each claim accordingly.
