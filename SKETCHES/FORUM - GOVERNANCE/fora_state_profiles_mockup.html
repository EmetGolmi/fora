◆ What a Governor Actually Does
The Governor of Pennsylvania is the chief executive of the Commonwealth. They sign or veto bills passed by the General Assembly, propose the annual state budget, command the Pennsylvania National Guard, appoint cabinet secretaries and judges, and issue executive orders. Unlike U.S. Senators or Representatives, the Governor doesn't vote on legislation — they respond to it. Understanding a governor's record means understanding what they signed, what they blocked, and how they allocated the state's money.

◆ Executive Authority
Executive Orders & Actions
Executive orders don't require legislative approval — they are the governor's direct command. They can create policy, reorganize agencies, and respond to emergencies without waiting for the General Assembly.

On his first day in office, Shapiro signed an executive order removing college degree requirements from 92% of state government jobs. No vote needed — just a signature. This is executive power in action.

◆ What a State Senator Does
Pennsylvania's 50-member State Senate is the upper chamber of the General Assembly. Senators serve 4-year terms, represent larger geographic districts than House members, and have more time between elections — which means less direct pressure from voters and more room to pursue longer-term legislation. Senate approval is required for all bills before they reach the Governor. A senator's committee assignments reveal where their real power sits — committees decide which bills live and which die.
◆ Why This District Matters for Philadelphia
District 1 covers South Philadelphia, Center City, and parts of the Riverwards — the heart of Philadelphia's densest neighborhoods. Saval chairs the Philadelphia Senate Delegation, meaning he coordinates all Philadelphia senators on issues affecting the city. His housing, climate, and criminal justice work directly shapes the laws Philadelphians live under.

◆ What a State Representative Does
Pennsylvania's 203-member House of Representatives is the lower chamber — the one closest to the people. Representatives serve 2-year terms and represent smaller districts than senators, which means they face voters more often and are more directly accountable. Every bill must pass both the House and Senate before reaching the Governor. House members introduce more bills, move faster, and are your most accessible point of contact in Harrisburg.
◆ 2-Year Terms — Why That Matters to You
Waxman is up for re-election in November 2026 — the same ballot as the Governor's race. Every 2 years, House members make the case to their district that their work was worth it. This is the most direct democratic accountability in Pennsylvania's government. If you live in District 182, your vote on his race is guaranteed to matter.
